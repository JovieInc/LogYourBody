# Training frequency and splits

Use these facts as evidence context. Do not turn a source fact into an individualized prescription.

- A muscle's training frequency can be based on how long it takes to recover after its own hard training dose. [k:0f907350]
- Resolution of soreness and return of performance to normal or above are indicators of recovery before training again. [k:18af6f5c]
- Muscles can recover at different rates after comparable relative training volume. [k:28d02fe6]
- An illustrative frequency arrangement placed quadriceps at twice weekly and biceps at four times weekly to reflect different recovery times. [k:2beb5da6]
- Increasing a faster-recovering muscle's per-session volume to match a slower-recovering muscle's schedule can push it near its recovery limit and may require repeated volume reductions or recovery phases. [k:67089b20]
- General rankings of faster- and slower-recovering muscles do not apply in every case; individual recovery times matter. [k:764e0844]
- For most people, an example of six failure sets for each muscle group placed biceps recovery at about two to three days and quadriceps recovery at three to four days or longer. [k:a1116bd2]
- For many muscles, around 10 near-failure sets in one session was described as a point beyond which further sets may add more damage than benefit. [k:be0e4142]
- Larger muscles generally take longer to recover from a comparable stimulus. [k:ef663446]
