# Volume landmarks

Use these facts as evidence context. Do not turn a source fact into an individualized prescription.

- The relationship between training volume and muscle growth has diminishing returns. [k:17d0690c]
- Across roughly 5 to 35 sets per muscle per week, doubling volume was associated with about 50% more relative growth in the estimates described. [k:1b31bb63]
- For beginners and people returning to training, one to three sets per muscle per week can produce detectable gains, though not necessarily the best possible gains. [k:248d8110]
- A volume-accounting model that counted indirect compound work fractionally, such as a row as about half a biceps set, fit the hypertrophy data better than the alternatives described. [k:45b5a80f]
- Around two to three hard fractional sets per muscle per week were described as sufficient to maintain muscle mass in relatively untrained groups. [k:5985b36a]
- Greater weekly training volume generally leads to more muscle growth. [k:64822aea]
- Around four fractional sets per muscle per week were associated with detectable hypertrophy. [k:81c218db]
- Results for higher volume on an individual muscle group cannot by themselves establish whether that volume is feasible across a full-body program. [k:bc9ce13c]
- When recovery permits, volume increases can be made gradually. [k:ce092a1e]
- More than 20 sets per muscle per week was categorized as extremely high volume for most people. [k:ed46b889]
- Evidence above 30 sets per muscle per week was limited, and the described research had not established a plateau or decline through roughly 30 to 40 sets. [k:f1a38a31]
- Around 10 fractional sets per muscle per week were estimated to yield about 40% of maximum hypertrophy; this was presented as a rough estimate. [k:fb00cc0e]
