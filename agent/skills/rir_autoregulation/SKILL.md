# Reps in reserve and autoregulation

Use these facts as evidence context. Do not turn a source fact into an individualized prescription.

- People may substantially misjudge how close a set is to failure and leave more repetitions in reserve than they estimate. [k:053f531a]
- The described progression backs off, potentially with a deload, when failure is reached, performance cannot be matched, and performance begins to decline from fatigue. [k:0b3ea5bb]
- The beginner approach described starts at four or five repetitions in reserve and progresses to about two repetitions in reserve before recycling the process with more load. [k:1795aef0]
- A progression approach described starts a training period with a selected load, repetitions, and sets, initially leaving about three repetitions in reserve. [k:23b443cd]
- The transcript reports no clear benefit from routinely taking each set closer than about three repetitions from failure, compared with stopping roughly three repetitions short. [k:27cf4ce2]
- Training to absolute failure can create substantial fatigue that may interfere with subsequent workouts. [k:7c4ce140]
- The transcript advises against taking exercises with a risk of the load falling on the trainee all the way to failure. [k:8407b016]
- The described progression increases load while holding repetitions steady, increases repetitions while holding load steady, or combines both approaches over successive workouts. [k:88ea9e15]
- The transcript says advanced trainees may benefit from training slightly beyond failure and may require a higher minimum level of effort. [k:8caf6ad3]
- The transcript defines an intermediate training age as roughly two to seven years. [k:934b782c]
- The advanced progression described begins around two repetitions in reserve, decreases reserve across early weeks, and may include beyond-failure work before a deload in week four. [k:a4fdb88b]
- For beginners, the transcript prioritizes establishing technique and says very hard training is not necessary for progress. [k:bb8fae33]
- For intermediate trainees, the described progression starts around three repetitions in reserve and approaches zero to one repetitions in reserve within each training cycle. [k:c654ae16]
